# Global AI Industry Brief (ex-US, ex-China) — 2026-06-13

## Executive Summary
AI ecosystems outside the US and China are developing rapidly, with Europe focusing on regulatory leadership and industrial AI, the Middle East investing heavily in AI infrastructure, and Japan/Korea leveraging manufacturing and semiconductor strengths. The EU AI Act is now fully in force, shaping global AI governance norms. Cross-border AI supply chain dynamics are increasingly complex amid US-China technology tensions.

---

## Europe

### EU AI Act Implementation
- **Full enforcement began February 2026** for high-risk AI systems
- **General-purpose AI rules**: Transparency, risk assessment, and cybersecurity requirements for GPAI models above 10^25 FLOPs
- **EU AI Office**: Established; 200+ staff; coordinating enforcement across 27 member states
- **Impact**: Compliance costs significant for SMEs; open-source models granted partial exemptions
- European AI companies using regulation as competitive moat ("trustworthy AI" branding)

### European AI Champions
- **Mistral AI (France)**: Mistral Large 3 released Q1 2026; strong in multilingual European use cases. Valued at ~$8B (Series D, March 2026). Open-weight strategy competing with Meta's Llama.
- **Aleph Alpha (Germany)**: Sovereign AI focus; government and defense contracts. €500M+ in public/private funding.
- **SAP**: Embedding AI across enterprise ERP suite; Joule copilot reaching 50M+ users.
- **Siemens**: Industrial AI for manufacturing, energy, and healthcare; "Industrial Copilot" deployed in 200+ factories.

### National AI Strategies
- **France**: "France 2030" AI investment plan; €2.5B committed; Mistral as national champion
- **Germany**: AI in manufacturing (Industrie 4.0); €5B over 5 years for AI research
- **UK**: AI Safety Institute (AISI) now testing frontier models pre-deployment; £1B AI sector deal
- **EU**: €10B AI investment through Horizon Europe and Digital Europe programmes (2025-2027)

## Asia-Pacific (ex-China)

### Japan
- **AI Strategy**: "AI 2026" national plan emphasizing robotics integration, healthcare AI, and semiconductor revival
- **Rapidus**: Japan's advanced chip foundry project; 2nm process target by 2027; ¥5 trillion government backing
- **SoftBank**: $100B+ AI investment vehicle (IZANAGI project); ARM-based AI edge computing push
- **Preferred Networks**: Japan's leading AI startup; industrial AI and robotics focus
- **Corporate AI**: Toyota, Hitachi, Sony embedding AI across product lines

### South Korea
- **Samsung**: Foundry business competing for AI chip orders; 2nm GAA process development. HBM4 memory leadership (80%+ market share for AI GPU memory).
- **SK Hynix**: HBM4 supply to NVIDIA; $40B+ investment in memory fabs through 2028
- **Naver**: HyperCLOVA X 2.0 — Korea's sovereign LLM; government and enterprise adoption
- **Government**: $20B "K-Semiconductor" strategy; AI Act modeled on EU framework under consideration

### India
- **AI Mission**: ₹10,000 crore (~$1.2B) for AI computing infrastructure and startups
- **Bhashini**: National language AI project; models covering 22 official Indian languages
- **Startup ecosystem**: Krutrim (Ola), Sarvam AI raising significant rounds; enterprise AI adoption in IT services (TCS, Infosys, Wipro deploying AI at scale)
- **Data center boom**: Reliance, Adani, AWS, Google investing $15B+ in Indian AI cloud capacity

## Cross-Border Dynamics

### AI Supply Chain
- **Memory (HBM)**: Korea (Samsung, SK Hynix) dominates; critical dependency for all AI accelerators
- **Advanced Packaging**: Taiwan (TSMC CoWoS) remains bottleneck; expansion underway
- **Foundry**: TSMC (Taiwan) 3nm/2nm for AI chips; geopolitical risk premium
- **Power**: Singapore, Malaysia emerging as regional AI data center hubs

### AI Governance Fragmentation
- EU: Precautionary, rights-based (AI Act)
- UK: Pro-innovation, sectoral approach
- Japan/Korea: Business-friendly, minimal regulation
- UAE/Saudi: State-directed, development-focused
- Growing divergence creating compliance complexity for global AI companies

## Investment Implications

### Opportunities
- **HBM memory**: Samsung, SK Hynix — structural demand from AI accelerators
- **European AI software**: SAP, Siemens — industrial AI applications with deep moats
- **Indian IT services**: TCS, Infosys — AI-driven productivity and new service lines
- **Japanese semiconductor equipment**: Tokyo Electron, Lasertec — advanced packaging tools
- **Middle East AI infrastructure**: Data centers, energy, connectivity

### Risks
- EU AI Act compliance costs disadvantaging European startups vs. US/China
- Geopolitical risk around Taiwan (TSMC) — no near-term alternative for advanced AI chips
- Fragmented regulation increasing global deployment complexity
- Middle East AI investments dependent on sustained oil revenues
